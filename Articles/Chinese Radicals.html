Chinese Radicals

This only explains radicals in Mandarin

Introduction

In the many dialects of the Chinese language, chinese radicals are essentially the building blocks of the many, many words within their own dialect.

Components of a Character

A Chinese character is broken up into many components which are groups of strokes/lines used to form a character with a specific meaning. Components tell a general meaning on their own like 女 (Nǚ) which translates to "woman" while some characters called compound characters are made out of multiple components like 妈妈 (Māmā / Mom) which is made out of both 女 (Nǚ) and 马 (Mǎ)...

Different Types of Components

Radicals in Mandarin are considered components since they make up some characters. But not all components that make up a character tell the meaning of it through it's own meaning, rather how it's said gives a clue on what the character means. Like 妈妈 (Māmā), in it, 女 (Nǚ) means "woman", while 马 (Mǎ), means horse, which at first doesn't make sense, but when the two components are added together, the way how it's said makes sense... 女 (Nǚ) 马 (Mǎ) → 妈 (mā) → 妈 (Māmā)

Dialect: A form of a language unique to a specific region. (Ex. Mandarin, Cantonese)

Radicals (Chinese): A character that is often seen as a component to create new, more specific characters

Character: A drawing that tells the meaning, or means something
